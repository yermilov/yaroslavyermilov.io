abstract: >-
  Loops, skills, artifacts, factory. AI conferences love words like these, and then leave them on
  the slide. Let's skip the hype and cash them in. I'll take them apart one by
  one — and I won't stop at four; I'll unpack a whole stack of AI buzzwords on real, practical
  tasks, not dry theory.


  One worked example runs throughout: a personal AI assistant I've spent the past year building.
  It isn't a chatbot. It's a task queue with a roster of specialised agents that claim work, do
  it, and report back. Through that single build the jargon turns into concrete machinery. Loops
  re-run my recurring chores. Skills are just markdown files that teach an agent how I book my car
  or file my taxes. Artifacts are what one agent leaves behind so the next doesn't start blind.
  And the factory is what you get when all of it runs on its own: a line of agents that turns a
  queue of chores into finished work.


  I haven't done my own life admin by hand in months. Car service, water meters, coffee re-orders,
  vinyl hunts, buying euros, power-outage graphs — the agents handle it. They even maintain
  themselves: every night they scan their own error logs, catch performance regressions, and
  rewrite their own skills. Some experiments worked brilliantly; others failed spectacularly. I'll
  show you both — including how this very talk description got brainstormed and submitted by the
  assistant it's about.
