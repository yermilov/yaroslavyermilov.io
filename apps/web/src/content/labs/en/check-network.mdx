So I dropped the link into my Codex and asked it to do the same for me.

My work Zoom calls kept dropping. I decided to find out whether an agent could fix it. I pay for a gigabit, and I had no idea what actually reached my desk. I didn't know how to measure it either. Those nine days turned into the `check-network` skill.

## Try it yourself

Claude Code:

```
/plugin marketplace add yermilov/learn-yy-skills
/plugin install home-it@learn-yy-skills
```

In Codex there's no command to look up — launch it and just ask: "install the marketplace `yermilov/learn-yy-skills` and the `home-it` plugin from it". It takes it from there.

Or take the single skill into any agent, no marketplace ([`npx skills`](https://github.com/vercel-labs/skills) supports Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and 60+ others):

```bash
npx skills add yermilov/learn-yy-skills --skill check-network
```

Then just say "use check-network to make my home network faster".

## What it found

75 Mbps. The next run gave 190. Then eighty-something again. At my desk the speed just jumped from run to run.

The agent ruled out the usual causes one by one. The plan is already the top tier. DNS answers in 10 ms. MTU is fine, zero interface errors. But the ping to my own router was losing 5% of packets, and that ended the hunt. If packets die inside the apartment, the ISP has nothing to do with it.

The cause was distance. Nothing exotic: the laptop was talking straight to the router, just across the whole apartment. −68 dBm, and the connection speed topped out at 234 Mbps. That is the ceiling, four times below my plan. The gigabit never reached my desk at all. macOS shows the signal and the connection speed right in the Wi-Fi status — no speed test needed.

The cheapest diagnostic in the world: pick up the laptop and walk five meters to the router. −20 dBm instead of −68, an 866 Mbps link, 624 Mbps, zero loss.

<figure>
  <img src="/lab/check-network/2-next-to-router.jpg" alt="Next to the router: 624 Mbps, 0% packet loss, −20 dBm, link 866 Mbps" width="1360" height="765" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

The two things the internet tells you to do first did nothing. Turning off Smart Connect: 611 vs 612 Mbps. Picking "cleaner" channels by hand: 398 on channel 36, 418 on channel 44 — and a re-measurement on auto in the same session gave 382. It is all one flat band of noise: the channel decides nothing.

I asked an LLM council (ChatGPT + Gemini) whether to buy a second extender for the desk. Both ranked that option last: an extender that is itself sitting on Wi-Fi halves the bandwidth — one hand holding the link to the router, the other to the laptop. The right fix is a cable from the router to the desk with the extender in access-point mode.

I never ran that cable. I plugged the Mac into the extender's gigabit port — the same extender still hanging off a wireless backhaul — so roughly 80% of the fix. 492 Mbps, zero loss.

The agent honestly noted in its report that this is not a real wired backhaul and that 800–940 Mbps is still on the table.

500 Mbps is quite good already. I stopped there.
